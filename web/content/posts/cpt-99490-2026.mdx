## What CPT 99490 covers

**CPT 99490** reimburses physician practices for at least 20 minutes of
non-face-to-face clinical staff time, directed by a physician, NP, or
PA, delivered to a patient with two or more chronic conditions expected
to last at least twelve months. It&rsquo;s the base code of every
Chronic Care Management program on Medicare. If you&rsquo;re billing
CCM at all, you&rsquo;re billing 99490.

The 2026 national-average Medicare rate is approximately **$66 per
enrolled patient per month**. Actual amounts vary by Medicare locality
(the geographic adjustment) and payer contract. A 200-patient panel
fully enrolled at the base code therefore nets roughly **$13,200 per
month** to the practice — the figure most CCM pitches lead with.

## What has to be true for 99490 to bill cleanly

Five conditions, every one required:

1. **The patient has two or more chronic conditions** expected to last
   at least twelve months (hypertension, Type 2 diabetes, COPD, CHF,
   CKD, depression, arthritis, dementia — the list is broad).
2. **The patient is enrolled in Medicare Part B.**
3. **Documented consent is on file** — the patient has agreed to
   receive CCM services, understands only one practitioner can bill
   CCM for them at a time, understands there may be cost-sharing, and
   can revoke at any time.
4. **A comprehensive care plan exists** in the EHR — problem list,
   treatment goals, medication management, coordination with outside
   providers.
5. **At least 20 minutes of clinical staff time** has been delivered
   in the calendar month, directed by the billing physician &ldquo;incident to&rdquo;
   supervision, and documented with a time log.

Miss any one of those and the claim is at risk.

## The 20-minute floor is just that — a floor

20 minutes is the threshold to bill the base code. Most well-run
programs spend 25–35 minutes per patient per month in practice —
reconciling meds across specialists, following up on specialist
appointments the patient missed, calling before refills run out,
updating the care plan. The 20 minutes is a CMS billing requirement,
not a service-level ceiling.

If you&rsquo;re regularly going past 20 minutes, that&rsquo;s where the
add-on code comes in.

## CPT 99439 — the add-on

**CPT 99439** reimburses each additional 20-minute increment of clinical
staff time in the same calendar month for the same patient. 2026 rate
is approximately **$46**. You can bill 99439 up to twice per patient
per month, which lifts the per-patient-per-month revenue by ~70%
when the clinical complexity justifies it.

The common pattern:

- Base (99490) — most enrolled patients, 20–25 min
- Base + 1 add-on (99490 + 99439) — patients with a recent hospital
  discharge, a specialist handoff, or an active medication change
- Base + 2 add-ons — rare; most of this volume is better billed under
  Complex CCM (99487 / 99489)

## What auditors actually look at

Medicare contractors audit CCM billing more often than practices
expect. The three things that get programs clawed back:

- **Time logs that aren&rsquo;t specific.** &ldquo;Reviewed care&rdquo; for 20
  minutes doesn&rsquo;t cut it. Logs should name the activity
  (medication reconciliation, specialist follow-up, caregiver call)
  and the clinical context.
- **Missing or late consent.** Consent must precede the first billed
  month, documented in the chart. A retroactive consent won&rsquo;t
  survive an audit.
- **Stale care plans.** A care plan written at enrollment and never
  updated reads as pro-forma. CMS expects the plan to evolve with the
  patient — quarterly updates at minimum, more often when the clinical
  picture changes.
