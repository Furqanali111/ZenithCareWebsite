Principal care management (PCM) is the monthly care management service for a patient whose main
problem is **one serious condition**, not a long list. CPT 99426 and 99427 are the two PCM codes
for **clinical staff time**. This guide explains the time rules, who can perform the work, who
qualifies, and what the record must show.

It is based on CMS's June 2025
[Chronic Care Management booklet](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/chroniccaremanagement.pdf)
(MLN909188) and the AAFP's
[summary of the PCM codes](https://www.aafp.org/fpm/2022/0100/p9). CMS's summary of the CY 2026
Physician Fee Schedule final rule (effective January 1, 2026) highlights no change to these time
rules, but codes and policies change every year, so confirm the current requirements before you bill.

## What are CPT 99426 and 99427?

**Short answer:** 99426 is billed for the first 30 minutes of clinical staff time in a calendar
month. 99427 is the add-on for each additional 30 minutes, up to two times per month.

| Code | Who performs the time | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| 99424 | Physician or other qualified health care professional | First 30 minutes per month |
| +99425 | Physician or other qualified health care professional | Each additional 30 minutes |
| 99426 | Clinical staff, under the clinician's direction | First 30 minutes per month |
| +99427 | Clinical staff, under the clinician's direction | Each additional 30 minutes (up to twice per month) |

## How do the time thresholds work?

PCM is time based, and the month is a calendar month. Time does not carry over.

| Documented clinical staff time | What can be reported |
|---|---|
| Under 30 minutes | Nothing for PCM that month |
| 30 to 59 minutes | 99426 |
| 60 to 89 minutes | 99426 plus 99427 once |
| 90 minutes or more | 99426 plus 99427 twice (the maximum) |

Never count the same minutes toward two billed services.

## Who qualifies for PCM?

CMS describes PCM for a patient with **one high-risk chronic condition expected to last at least 3
months** that puts them at significant risk of hospitalization, acute exacerbation or
decompensation, functional decline, or death. In practice, the condition should also:

- Require a care plan that is developed, monitored or revised
- Need frequent medication adjustments or unusually complex management
- Involve ongoing coordination among the clinicians treating the patient

CMS also requires another initial visit after one year to continue PCM services.

If the patient has two or more chronic conditions, the usual fit is
[chronic care management](/services/chronic-care-management) instead. See
[CCM vs. RPM vs. PCM](/resources/ccm-vs-rpm-vs-pcm) for how to choose.

## What does the record need to show?

The documentation habits are close to CCM. For each patient and month, keep:

- **A qualifying condition,** with why it carries significant risk
- **A current care plan** for that condition, and the date it was last reviewed or revised
- **A time log:** the date, minutes, who did the work (name and role) and what was done
- **Evidence of coordination** with the other clinicians involved, such as the specialist and the
  primary care practitioner
- **Supervision:** the billing practitioner directs the clinical staff

Our [time tracking guide](/resources/ccm-time-tracking-requirements) shows what auditors look for
in a time log, and the [CPT 99490 documentation checklist](/resources/cpt-99490-documentation-checklist)
covers the CCM version of the same record.

## What are the common mistakes?

- **Billing 99426 below 30 minutes.** There is no partial credit. Under 30 minutes, nothing is
  reported.
- **Billing 99427 a third time.** The add-on stops at two units per month.
- **Counting the same minutes twice,** for example toward PCM and another care management service.
- **Using PCM for a patient who really has multiple chronic conditions,** where CCM fits better.
- **Assuming PCM and CCM stack.** The same practitioner generally cannot report both for one patient
  in one month.
