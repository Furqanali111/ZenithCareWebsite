Choosing a chronic care management (CCM) vendor is mostly a question of **who does the work, who bills,
and who keeps the revenue**. The vendors you are comparing, ChartSpan included, may differ on all
three, and pricing is often quoted per practice rather than published. This guide gives you a
neutral way to compare them. It does not rank or describe any vendor's terms, because those change
and you should get them in writing.

## What are the common ways to run a CCM program?

**Short answer:** there are four, and the right one depends on your staff, your panel size and how much
control you want.

| Model | Who does the monthly work | How you pay | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| In-house team | Your own clinical staff | Salaries and benefits | Hiring, turnover and time-log discipline |
| Revenue-share service | The vendor's staff | A percentage of what you collect for CCM | The percentage grows with enrollment; check the exit terms |
| Flat-fee staffing | The vendor's staff, inside your EHR | A fixed monthly invoice | Scope and the minimum term |
| Software only | Your staff, with the vendor's tool | A subscription | You still need the people to do the calls |

See [in-house vs. outsourced CCM](/resources/in-house-vs-outsourced-ccm) for how the first choice
compares with the rest.

## What do the numbers look like?

The two paid models can cost very different amounts as your program grows. Use your own numbers.
Here is an illustration with made-up terms (not any vendor's real price): at the 2026 national
average of about $66 per enrolled patient per month for CPT 99490 (rates vary by locality, see our
[CPT 99490 guide](/resources/cpt-99490-2026)), 200 enrolled patients bring in roughly $13,200 a month.
A hypothetical 35% revenue share would be about $4,620 of that, and it rises as you enroll more patients.
A flat fee stays the same at 200 patients or 400. Actual results vary by practice, so run both
calculations at your own enrollment before you decide.

## 12 questions to ask every vendor

Ask all of them the same questions and ask for the answers in writing.

1. **Who bills Medicare?** Your practitioner should bill under their own supervision, and the
   vendor's staff work under general supervision. Get this stated plainly.
2. **What exactly do we pay, and how does it change?** Fixed fee, percentage, setup fees, minimums.
3. **Who employs and trains the staff?** Where are they based, and what clinical background do they
   have?
4. **Do the staff work inside our EHR?** Or in a separate system that we cannot see?
5. **How is time logged?** Each entry needs a date, minutes, who did it and what was done. See our
   [time tracking guide](/resources/ccm-time-tracking-requirements).
6. **How is consent captured and stored?** Patients must be told about CCM, cost sharing, one
   practitioner per month and their right to stop. See the
   [consent requirements guide](/resources/ccm-consent-requirements).
7. **Who builds and maintains the care plan?** It must be electronic and shared with the patient.
8. **What is the 24/7 access arrangement?** CMS expects patients to reach a practitioner or clinical
   staff for urgent needs at any hour.
9. **Will you sign a Business Associate Agreement?** Before any patient data is touched, with no
   exceptions. See the [HIPAA checklist](/resources/hipaa-checklist-outsourced-ccm).
10. **What reports do we get, and how often?** Monthly per-patient time and activity summaries that
    your billing team can attach to the claim.
11. **What happens if we leave?** Notice period, return of records, and what happens to enrolled
    patients.
12. **Can we speak to a practice like ours?** Ask for references in your specialty and size.

Use the [CPT 99490 documentation checklist](/resources/cpt-99490-documentation-checklist) to test whether
a vendor's records would hold up when you bill.

## What are the red flags?

- A vendor who will not say in writing who bills or how fees are calculated
- No Business Associate Agreement, or one offered only after you sign
- Time logs that are summaries rather than dated entries
- A long minimum term with no way out if enrollment disappoints
- Revenue promises with no "results vary" caveat
