Medicare gives physician practices five distinct monthly care-management programs. Most practices have heard of
[Chronic Care Management](/services/chronic-care-management), but fewer know the full set or how to pick the
right starting point. This guide compares all five side by side so you can match each program to the patients
already on your panel.

## What are the five Medicare care management programs?

CMS created five recurring monthly programs that pay practices for non-face-to-face clinical staff time spent
keeping high-risk patients on track between office visits:

1. **Chronic Care Management (CCM):** for patients with two or more chronic conditions
2. **Principal Care Management (PCM):** for patients with one serious, high-risk condition
3. **Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM):** for patients transmitting physiologic device readings
4. **Remote Therapeutic Monitoring (RTM):** for patients using non-physiologic monitoring devices
5. **Transitional Care Management (TCM):** a one-time episode payment per hospital or SNF discharge

Each program has its own eligibility criteria, CPT codes, and documentation requirements. They are designed to
be complementary: a single panel can run several at once, and some patients qualify for more than one.

## How do the five programs compare side by side?

| Program | Core eligibility | Billing basis | Key CPT codes | Approx. 2026 rate (national avg.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CCM** | 2+ chronic conditions, 12+ months, Medicare Part B | Monthly per enrolled patient | 99490, 99439, 99487, 99489 | ~$66/mo base (99490) |
| **PCM** | 1 complex, high-risk condition (3+ months) | Monthly per enrolled patient | 99424, 99425, 99426, 99427 | ~$68-$88/mo (99426/99424) |
| **RPM** | Any condition requiring remote physiologic monitoring | Monthly per enrolled patient | 99453, 99454, 99457, 99458 | ~$50-$55/mo ongoing (99457) |
| **RTM** | Non-physiologic device monitoring (PT, respiratory, pain) | Monthly per enrolled patient | 98975, 98976, 98977, 98980, 98981 | ~$55-$75/mo |
| **TCM** | Discharge from inpatient/SNF in past 30 days | Per discharge episode | 99495, 99496 | ~$170-$290/episode |

All figures are 2026 national averages. Actual reimbursement varies by Medicare locality and geographic
adjustment factor. Verify current rates against the most recent Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule.

## Is CCM the right fit for a high chronic-condition panel?

CCM is the right starting point for most primary care, internal medicine, and geriatric practices. The
eligibility bar, two or more chronic conditions expected to last at least twelve months, is cleared by a
large fraction of any established Medicare panel.

Common qualifying conditions include hypertension, Type 2 diabetes, COPD, heart failure, hyperlipidemia,
chronic kidney disease, depression, anxiety, and osteoarthritis. In a typical primary care practice, an
initial panel screen often identifies 25 to 40 percent of Medicare patients as eligible.

The key billing requirement is **documented consent** before the first billed month, a **written care plan**
in the EHR, and **at least 20 minutes of clinical staff time** per calendar month logged with a time
record. The work is "incident to" physician supervision and can be performed by RNs, LPNs, MAs, or
other qualified clinical staff, which makes it practical to delegate or outsource.

For a detailed breakdown of billing mechanics and the 2026 CPT rates, see our
[CPT 99490 guide](/resources/cpt-99490-2026).

## When does RPM make more sense than CCM?

[Remote Patient Monitoring](/services/remote-patient-monitoring) (see the [RPM setup guide](/resources/rpm-setup-guide)) adds a device layer on top of the monthly
care-coordination workflow. The patient must use a connected monitoring device (typically a cellular blood
pressure cuff, glucometer, weight scale, or pulse oximeter) and transmit readings on at least 16 days in a
30-day period.

RPM is the natural add-on for CCM programs managing a lot of hypertension, heart failure, or diabetes
patients who are willing to use home devices. The monitoring data creates a clinical record that is both
clinically valuable and an audit trail: readings above threshold trigger documented interventions.

RPM and CCM can run for the same patient concurrently. Time cannot be double-counted across both codes,
but the device-monitoring overhead and the care-coordination work are distinct services. Many practices
layer RPM on top of their CCM program for their highest-risk patients.

## Which patients are right for PCM?

[Principal Care Management](/services/principal-care-management) is designed for specialists managing a
single complex, high-risk condition. Where CCM requires two or more conditions, PCM is built around one
condition that dominates the clinical picture and requires ongoing intensive management.

A cardiologist managing advanced heart failure, a nephrologist managing stage 4 CKD, or an
endocrinologist managing a patient with uncontrolled Type 1 diabetes all fit the PCM profile. The
condition must be expected to last at least three months and must require regular assessment and clinical
intervention.

PCM reimburses at roughly the same level per 20-minute increment as CCM. The same care-plan and time-log
requirements apply. One important constraint: the same provider cannot bill both PCM and CCM for the same
patient in the same calendar month. However, a patient's specialist can bill PCM for one condition while
the primary care physician separately bills CCM for the patient's other conditions.

## When do RTM and TCM apply?

[Remote Therapeutic Monitoring](/services/remote-therapeutic-monitoring) is the non-physiologic counterpart
to RPM. It covers monitoring of respiratory therapy adherence, pain management, physical therapy exercise
compliance, and similar non-vital-sign outcomes. RTM is most relevant for pulmonology, orthopedics, and
physical therapy practices.

[Transitional Care Management](/services/transitional-care-management) is structured differently from the
other four programs. It is not a recurring monthly payment but a one-per-episode payment tied to a hospital
or SNF discharge. TCM pays for a structured follow-up in the 30 days after discharge, including at minimum
one direct contact within two days and a face-to-face visit within 7 or 14 days depending on complexity.
See the [TCM billing and workflow guide](/resources/transitional-care-management-guide) for the full requirements.

The TCM episode reimbursement (roughly $170 to $290 depending on complexity and visit timing) is
significant for practices that see a steady volume of hospital discharges.

## Can a practice bill multiple programs for the same patient?

Yes, with constraints:

- **CCM and RPM together:** allowed. Both programs must be separately documented; time cannot be
  double-counted.
- **CCM and TCM in the same month:** allowed. They address different services.
- **CCM and PCM, same provider, same month:** not allowed. CMS prohibits the same billing provider from
  claiming both for the same patient in the same calendar month.
- **PCM and RPM together:** generally allowed. Confirm with your MAC for the current interpretation.

CMS updates billing rules annually. Your billing lead should verify any code combinations against the
current Medicare Program Integrity Manual before submitting.

## How should a practice decide where to start?

Start with the program that covers the most of your existing panel with the least operational change. For
most primary care, internal medicine, and geriatric practices, that is CCM.

A practical decision framework:

| Practice type | Best starting program | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Primary care or internal medicine (large Medicare panel) | CCM | Broadest eligibility, highest volume |
| Geriatrics | CCM, then Complex CCM (99487/99489) | Near-universal eligibility, high complex-CCM yield |
| Specialist (cardiology, nephrology, endocrinology) | PCM | Single-condition focus matches the specialist workflow |
| Any practice with device-monitoring patients | RPM added to CCM | Device data strengthens documentation |
| Any practice with hospital-discharge patients | TCM | High per-episode value with existing patients |
