Time tracking is the operational core of [Chronic Care Management](/services/chronic-care-management).
The monthly reimbursement depends on documented clinical staff time: if the time is not in the
record, it does not count toward the billing threshold, and the claim will not survive an audit.

This guide covers what time counts toward CCM, who can log it, how auditors review the documentation,
and the most common tracking mistakes to avoid.

## Why does time tracking matter for CCM?

Unlike most Medicare evaluation and management codes, CCM reimbursement is explicitly time-based.
CPT 99490 requires at least 20 minutes of qualifying clinical staff time in a calendar month. CPT
99439 (the add-on code) requires each additional 20-minute increment to be separately documented.
See the full breakdown in our [CPT 99490 vs. 99439 guide](/resources/cpt-99439-vs-99490).

The time log is the primary piece of evidence in a Medicare audit. If a practice submits a claim
for 99490 and the patient's record contains 12 minutes of documented activity, the claim will be
denied on review and the payment recouped. The documentation must exist before the claim is
submitted, not be reconstructed afterward.

## What counts toward the 20-minute threshold?

CMS defines qualifying CCM time as non-face-to-face clinical staff time spent on activities that
directly support the management of the patient's chronic conditions. Qualifying activities include:

**Communication activities:**
- Telephone calls with the patient or their designated caregiver about care coordination
- Secure messaging or patient portal communication related to chronic condition management
- Callbacks to follow up on symptoms, medication side effects, or missed appointments

**Clinical support activities:**
- Medication reconciliation and refill authorization
- Reviewing and updating the patient's care plan
- Care gap closure (ensuring overdue screenings or labs are scheduled)
- Education related to the patient's chronic conditions and self-management goals

**Coordination activities:**
- Communication with specialist offices about referrals or shared care
- Following up on lab or imaging results and communicating them to the patient
- Coordination with home health agencies, social workers, or community resources

**What does not count:**
- Time spent during a face-to-face office visit (that time is captured under the E/M code)
- Administrative tasks such as scheduling, prior authorizations unrelated to chronic conditions,
  or billing and coding work
- Time spent by non-clinical staff (front desk, billing coordinators)
- Duplicate time logged for the same activity under both CCM and Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM)

The calendar month resets on the first day of each month. Time cannot be carried over from one
month to the next.

## Who can perform and log CCM time?

CCM work must be performed by clinical staff. The "incident to" billing rule allows clinical staff
to provide CCM services under the general supervision of the billing physician or qualified
non-physician practitioner. The physician does not need to be physically present during each CCM
activity, but must be immediately available and must maintain general oversight of the care plan.

Clinical staff who can perform and document CCM time:
- Registered Nurses (RNs)
- Licensed Practical Nurses (LPNs)
- Certified Medical Assistants (CMAs) and Medical Assistants (MAs), depending on state scope
  of practice
- Licensed Clinical Social Workers (LCSWs), when their activities relate to care coordination
- Pharmacists embedded in the practice, for medication management activities

Non-clinical staff, including front-desk staff, billing coordinators, and scheduling staff, cannot
log time toward the CCM threshold regardless of the activity they performed.

Physicians, nurse practitioners, and physician assistants can also log CCM time directly. In
practices where the provider personally performs care coordination calls, the time is documented
in the provider's note rather than a clinical staff log.

## What do auditors look for in CCM documentation?

Medicare auditors reviewing CCM claims are looking for three things:

**1. Time specificity.** Each entry in the time log must show the date, the duration in minutes,
and who performed the activity. A note that says "Care coordination: 25 min" without a date or
staff member name is not sufficient.

**2. Activity specificity.** The log must show what was done, not just that time was spent. "Called
patient" is weak. "Called patient to review blood pressure log from home monitoring device.
Patient reported readings averaging 148/88 for past week. Reviewed current antihypertensive
regimen, reinforced medication adherence, scheduled follow-up call for next week. Updated care
plan note." is defensible.

**3. Cumulative time matching the billed code.** If 99490 + 99439 are billed (40 minutes), the
auditor will add up all the monthly log entries and verify the total reaches 40 minutes. If the
total is 28 minutes, the 99439 unit will be denied.

A common audit finding is a time log with entries that use identical language across patients.
Cookie-cutter documentation, where every patient's log for the month reads identically, flags
as potentially fabricated and triggers closer review.

## What are the most common time tracking mistakes?

**Logging time in aggregate at month-end.** Some practices have staff retroactively estimate how
long they spent on CCM activities across the month and enter it as a single lump-sum entry.
This is not compliant. Each activity should be logged at the time it occurs, with a specific date
and duration.

**Rounding up to the billing threshold.** If 17 minutes are documented, the correct action is to
do 3 more minutes of qualifying work and document it, or to not bill that month. Inflating the
logged time to reach 20 minutes is a compliance violation.

**Counting office visit time.** If the patient comes in for a visit and the provider spends time
on care coordination during that visit, that time is already captured under the E/M code for the
visit and cannot be counted toward the CCM threshold.

**No documentation for phone calls.** Some practices perform CCM phone calls but fail to document
them in the EHR at the time of the call. If the call is not in the record within a reasonable
time of occurring, it may not be defensible in an audit.

**Using CCM time for RPM.** If the practice is billing both CCM and Remote Patient Monitoring for
the same patient, the same minutes cannot count toward both. Each program must have its own
independently documented time.

## What tools and workflows support accurate CCM logging?

The simplest compliant approach is a structured EHR template that prompts the staff member to
enter the date, activity type, duration, and a brief narrative for each CCM interaction. Most
modern EHRs (Epic, Athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, Kareo) have a built-in CCM time-tracking module
or a configurable encounter type that supports this.

For practices using a dedicated CCM platform or outsourcing to a CCM vendor, the vendor's
platform should:

- Log each activity in real time, not retroactively
- Produce a monthly summary showing the cumulative documented time per patient
- Export to a format that can be attached to the claim or produced during audit

When reviewing a CCM vendor, ask to see a sample time log and audit report. If the vendor cannot
produce patient-level activity logs with dates, durations, and activity descriptions, the practice
is accepting the audit risk without the tools to defend it.
