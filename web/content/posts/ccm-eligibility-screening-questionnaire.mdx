Enrolling a patient who does not qualify is the most expensive CCM mistake, because every month
billed after that is exposed in an audit. A short, consistent screening questionnaire catches the
problem before the first claim. This one maps to the rules in CMS's
[Chronic Care Management booklet](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/chroniccaremanagement.pdf)
(MLN909188). Rules change each year, so confirm the current requirements with your billing team or
Medicare Administrative Contractor.

## What should a CCM eligibility questionnaire ask?

**Short answer:** whether the patient has Medicare, two or more qualifying chronic conditions, a
documented risk, a qualifying visit, no conflicting care-management billing, and a willingness to
consent. Use the ten questions below and file the answers in the chart.

### Coverage and conditions

1. **Is the patient covered by Medicare Part B?** Confirm coverage and any secondary payer before
   you enroll.
2. **Does the patient have two or more chronic conditions?** Each condition should be expected to
   last at least 12 months or until death. List them in the problem list.
3. **Do those conditions put the patient at significant risk?** The risk is of death, acute
   exacerbation or decompensation, or functional decline. Record the reason in the practitioner's
   own words where possible.
4. **Has the billing practitioner confirmed eligibility?** Staff can collect the facts. The
   practitioner makes the call.

### Visit history and billing conflicts

5. **Is the patient new, or not seen in the past year?** If so, an initiating visit is required.
6. **Was CCM discussed at that visit?** If it was not, the visit does not count as the initiating
   visit. See the [99490 documentation checklist](/resources/cpt-99490-documentation-checklist).
7. **Is another practitioner already billing CCM for this patient this month?** Only one
   practitioner can bill CCM in a calendar month.
8. **Is the patient in a program that overlaps with CCM?** Review [CCM vs. RPM vs. PCM](/resources/ccm-vs-rpm-vs-pcm)
   and [transitional care management](/resources/transitional-care-management-guide) for how the
   programs fit together.

### Consent and contact

9. **Has the patient been told what CCM involves?** That means cost sharing may apply, only one
   practitioner can bill per month, and they can stop at any time. See the
   [consent requirements guide](/resources/ccm-consent-requirements).
10. **Can staff reach the patient by phone each month?** CCM needs at least 20 minutes of logged
    clinical staff time per month, so a patient who cannot be reached will not generate a billable
    month. See [time tracking requirements](/resources/ccm-time-tracking-requirements).

## What do you do with the answers?

File the completed questionnaire in the chart, note who completed it and the date, and have the
practitioner sign off before consent is requested. Re-screen when the patient's conditions change
or they switch practitioners.
