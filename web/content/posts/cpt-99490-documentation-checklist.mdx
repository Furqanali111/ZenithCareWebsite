A CPT 99490 claim is only as strong as the patient record behind it. If any required piece is
missing on the day an auditor opens the chart, the monthly payment is at risk, even when the
work was done. This checklist lists what the record needs for each billed month, based on
CMS's June 2025 [Chronic Care Management booklet](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/chroniccaremanagement.pdf)
(MLN909188).

Use it as a pre-bill review. Rules and rates change, so confirm the current year's
requirements with your billing team or Medicare Administrative Contractor before relying on it.

## What does the record need to show before you bill CPT 99490?

**Short answer:** an eligible patient, a qualifying initiating visit, documented consent, a
comprehensive electronic care plan, at least 20 minutes of logged clinical staff time in the
calendar month, and general supervision by the billing practitioner.

| Requirement | Document this | One-time or monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | Two or more chronic conditions expected to last 12 months or until death, with significant risk of death, decompensation or functional decline | One-time, review as needed |
| Initiating visit | Date and type of the qualifying visit, with CCM discussed | One-time (new patients, or not seen in the past year) |
| Consent | Written or verbal consent, with the required disclosures | One-time per practitioner |
| Care plan | Electronic comprehensive care plan, kept current | Ongoing |
| Time log | Date, minutes, staff member and activity for each entry | Every month |
| Supervision | Billing practitioner directs the clinical staff | Every month |

## Is the patient eligible, and is it written down?

CCM is for patients with **two or more chronic conditions** expected to last at least 12 months
(or until death) that place them at significant risk of death, acute exacerbation or
decompensation, or functional decline.

- List the qualifying conditions in the problem list.
- Record why the patient is at risk, in the practitioner's own words where possible.
- Confirm Medicare eligibility before enrolling the patient.

## Did a qualifying initiating visit happen?

CMS requires an initiating visit **for new patients and for patients not seen in the previous
year**. It can be a comprehensive face-to-face E/M visit, an annual wellness visit (AWV) or an
initial preventive physical exam (IPPE).

- Record the date and visit type.
- Make sure CCM was discussed at that visit. If it was not, the visit does not count as the
  initiating visit.
- Keep in mind that the initiating visit is separately billable and is not part of CCM.

## Is consent documented?

Get the patient's written or verbal consent before you bill. In the medical record, document
that you told the patient:

- CCM services are available
- Cost sharing may apply
- Only one practitioner can furnish and bill CCM in a calendar month
- They can stop at any time, effective at the end of the calendar month
- That you explained these points, and whether the patient accepted or declined

Consent is obtained once, unless the patient switches to a different CCM practitioner. See our
[CCM consent requirements guide](/resources/ccm-consent-requirements) for wording and workflow
tips.

## Is there a current comprehensive care plan?

The care plan must be electronic, patient-centered and based on an assessment of the patient's
physical, mental, cognitive, psychosocial, functional and environmental needs. CMS lists these
elements a plan may include:

- Problem list
- Expected outcome and prognosis
- Measurable treatment goals
- Symptom management and planned interventions
- Medical management
- Cognitive and functional assessment, environmental evaluation and caregiver assessment
- Coordination with outside resources and practitioners
- Periodic review and revision

Make the plan available promptly inside and outside your practice, and give the patient or
caregiver a copy when needed. Record when you did so.

## Is the time logged correctly?

CPT 99490 covers the **first 20 minutes** of clinical staff time in a calendar month. CPT 99439
is the add-on for each additional 20 minutes, up to twice per month. For each log entry,
record:

- The date of the activity
- The minutes spent
- Who performed it (name and role)
- What was done, in specific terms rather than "care coordination"

Do not count the same minutes toward any other billed code, and do not carry time from one month
to the next. Our [time tracking guide](/resources/ccm-time-tracking-requirements) covers what
auditors look for in detail, and the [99490 vs. 99439 comparison](/resources/cpt-99439-vs-99490)
explains when to add the second code.

## Is supervision and staffing in order?

Clinical staff can furnish CCM "incident to" the billing practitioner, subject to state law,
licensure and scope of practice. CMS assigns the staff-delivered codes (99490, 99439, 99487,
99489) **general supervision**, so the practitioner does not need to be present, but the work must
happen under the practitioner's overall direction and control.

- Keep a record of which practitioner supervises the program.
- Make sure staff are employees or under contract with the billing practice.

## Does the program meet the service elements?

Beyond the claim itself, CMS describes the elements of a CCM program. Spot-check that you can
show:

- **Certified EHR use:** demographics, problems, medications and medication allergies recorded in
  certified EHR technology.
- **24/7 access:** patients can reach a practitioner or clinical staff for urgent needs at any
  time.
- **Continuity:** a designated care team member who stays in regular contact with the patient.
- **Care transitions:** follow-up after ED visits and discharges, and sharing of continuity of
  care documents.
- **Medication review and coordination** with home and community-based providers.

## What stops you from billing CPT 99490 in a month?

- You cannot bill non-complex CCM (99490, 99439) and complex CCM (99487, 99489) in the same
  calendar month for the same patient.
- CPT 99491 and 99437 (billing practitioner's own time) cannot be reported in the same
  calendar month as 99490, 99439, 99487 or 99489.
- CCM cannot be billed in the same service period as home health supervision (G0181), hospice
  supervision (G0182) or certain ESRD services (CPT 90951 to 90970).
- You can bill remote physiologic monitoring (RPM) or remote therapeutic monitoring (RTM) with
  CCM, but not both of them.
- CCM can be reported during a transitional care management period (99495, 99496), but the
  same time cannot count toward both services.
