Compliance officers evaluating a Chronic Care Management vendor get
exactly the same pitch from everyone: *&ldquo;We&rsquo;re HIPAA
compliant.&rdquo;* The phrase is marketing, not a checklist. Here are
the twelve concrete questions that separate real posture from
boilerplate, plus the red flags that mean walk away.

## Before you share any patient information

### 1. BAA, before or after?

**Ask:** &ldquo;When do you sign the Business Associate Agreement?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** before any PHI changes hands, as a prerequisite
to onboarding.
**Red flag:** &ldquo;We&rsquo;ll sign one once we&rsquo;re under
contract.&rdquo; HIPAA requires a signed BAA before the vendor touches
PHI, not after.

### 2. BAA sourced from where?

**Ask:** &ldquo;What&rsquo;s your BAA based on?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** HHS sample provisions or similar
industry-standard template, redlineable by your legal team.
**Red flag:** a vendor-authored BAA with unusual carve-outs,
indemnity language skewed heavily in their favor, or no willingness
to negotiate terms.

### 3. Minimum Necessary

**Ask:** &ldquo;How do you apply the Minimum Necessary rule?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** specific role-based access, no bulk exports, no
patient data moved to vendor-owned platforms.
**Red flag:** &ldquo;We pull the whole patient list into our platform
monthly.&rdquo; That&rsquo;s not Minimum Necessary, that&rsquo;s a
PHI export.

## How they handle data in motion

### 4. Where does the work happen?

**Ask:** &ldquo;What system do your staff work in?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** inside your EHR or your CCM platform under
delegated access.
**Red flag:** &ldquo;We export patient data to our internal CRM.&rdquo;
Every data movement is a surface area; a vendor working inside your
system has none.

### 5. Communication channels

**Ask:** &ldquo;How do your staff communicate with patients and with
us?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** HIPAA-eligible channels only (patient portal,
encrypted email under BAA, documented phone calls inside the EHR).
**Red flag:** regular SMS to patients, personal Gmail accounts, chat
tools without HIPAA coverage.

### 6. Email tooling

**Ask:** &ldquo;What email platform do your staff use, and do you have a
signed BAA with that provider?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, each on a
healthcare-eligible plan with the vendor&rsquo;s own BAA signed with
the email provider.
**Red flag:** free Gmail, non-business email, or inability to
produce the sub-processor BAA on request.

## Training and oversight

### 7. Workforce HIPAA training

**Ask:** &ldquo;How is your workforce trained on HIPAA?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** named training program, documented completion,
annual refresh, sanctions for violations.
**Red flag:** &ldquo;It&rsquo;s part of onboarding&rdquo; without
specifics.

### 8. Named contacts

**Ask:** &ldquo;Who is my privacy contact and who is my security
contact at your organization?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** two named humans with email addresses, same for
every client.
**Red flag:** &ldquo;Just email support.&rdquo; A compliance structure
without named owners is a structure that falls apart under audit.

## Breach handling

### 9. Breach notification SLA

**Ask:** &ldquo;If there&rsquo;s a breach, how fast do you tell us?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** without unreasonable delay and in no case later
than the HIPAA-required timeframe (60 days from discovery to the
covered entity under 45 CFR 164).
**Red flag:** vague answer, or a longer SLA than the regulation
allows.

### 10. Audit log access

**Ask:** &ldquo;Can we see your access logs on our patients&rsquo;
records?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** yes, on request, or via the EHR&rsquo;s native
audit log (since the vendor works inside your system).
**Red flag:** &ldquo;Our platform generates its own logs, we can send
excerpts.&rdquo; Excerpts aren&rsquo;t audit evidence.

## Business continuity

### 11. End-of-engagement

**Ask:** &ldquo;What happens to our patient data at the end of the
engagement?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** no vendor-held PHI to return or destroy, the
data stayed in your EHR the whole time. Access is revoked.
**Red flag:** a long &ldquo;return of PHI&rdquo; process. That means they
had an export of your PHI.

### 12. Sub-processors

**Ask:** &ldquo;Who are your sub-processors, any vendors with potential
access to PHI?&rdquo;
**Correct answer:** a short named list (email provider, EHR vendor
if applicable), each with a BAA on file.
**Red flag:** &ldquo;We don&rsquo;t have sub-processors&rdquo; (very
unlikely for any modern vendor) or inability to produce the list.

## The one meta-check that matters most

If the vendor can&rsquo;t answer any of these twelve questions
quickly and specifically, that&rsquo;s your answer. HIPAA posture
isn&rsquo;t a marketing position; it&rsquo;s an operational discipline
with written artifacts behind it. A vendor that has done the work can
produce the BAA, the sub-processor list, the training completion
records, and the privacy contact within one business day. A vendor
that hasn&rsquo;t will stall, hedge, or send you back to a sales rep.
