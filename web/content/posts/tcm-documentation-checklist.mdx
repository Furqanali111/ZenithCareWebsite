A Transitional Care Management (TCM) claim is easy to lose on paperwork. The care can be excellent
and the payment still fails if the chart cannot prove the contact date, the visit date or the
medication review. This checklist lists what the record needs for CPT 99495 and 99496, in the order
the work happens, so your team can check a chart before the claim goes out. It is the companion to our
[transitional care management guide](/resources/transitional-care-management-guide), which explains
how the program works. If you would rather not run the deadlines yourself, our
[transitional care management services](/services/transitional-care-management) team handles the
contact, follow-up and documentation inside your EHR.

The rules below follow CMS's
[Transitional Care Management Services](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mln908628-transitional-care-management-services.pdf)
guidance (MLN908628). This is educational, not billing advice. Rules change every year, so confirm
the current requirements with your billing lead or Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC).

## What does the record need to show before you bill TCM?

**Short answer:** a qualifying discharge, an interactive contact within 2 business days, the
non-face-to-face work, medication reconciliation by the visit date, and a face-to-face visit inside
the window that matches the level of medical decision making (MDM).

| Requirement | Document this | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Qualifying discharge | Discharge date, facility type, where the patient went | Day 0 |
| Interactive contact | Date, who made it, method, what was discussed, or each failed attempt | Within 2 business days |
| Non-face-to-face services | Discharge record review, follow-up on tests, referrals, education | During the 30 days |
| Medication reconciliation | Reconciled list, who did it, date | On or before the visit date |
| Face-to-face visit | Visit date and modality | Within 7 days (99496) or 14 days (99495) |
| Medical decision making | MDM level and the support for it | At the visit |

CMS's minimum is four items: the **discharge date**, the **date of the interactive contact**, the
**date of the face-to-face visit**, and the **complexity of MDM**. The rest of this list is what makes
those four hold up when someone reviews the chart.

## Was the discharge a qualifying one?

TCM starts with a discharge from one of these settings:

- Inpatient acute care hospital
- Inpatient psychiatric hospital
- Inpatient rehabilitation facility
- Long-term care hospital
- Skilled nursing facility or nursing facility
- Hospital outpatient observation or partial hospitalization
- Partial hospitalization at a community mental health center

The patient must go to a community setting: home, a domiciliary such as a group home, a rest home,
or an assisted living facility.

**Document:** the discharge date, the facility and setting type, the setting the patient returned
to, and how you learned about the discharge (hospital notice, health information exchange, patient
call). The discharge date starts every other clock, so write down its source.

## Is the interactive contact documented?

The contact is due **within 2 business days after discharge**. Business days are Monday through
Friday, not counting holidays, whatever your office hours are. A patient discharged on Friday must be
reached by the end of Tuesday.

- **Who:** the physician, a qualified nonphysician practitioner (NPP), or clinical staff under their
  direction.
- **How:** phone, electronic communication or in person.
- **What:** the contact has to address the patient's status and needs, not only book an appointment.
  Ask about symptoms, medications, equipment, services and how the patient will get to the visit.

**Document:** the date and time, the staff member, the method and a short summary of what was
discussed.

If you cannot reach the patient, CMS lets you report TCM after **two or more separate attempts** made
in a timely manner, when every other requirement is met. Log each attempt like this:

| Attempt | Date and time | Method | Staff | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mon 9:10 AM | Phone, patient's mobile | RN | No answer, voicemail left |
| 2 | Mon 3:40 PM | Phone, caregiver | RN | Reached caregiver, contact completed |

Keep trying after the second attempt. The attempts rule protects the claim, but the patient still
needs the call.

## Are the non-face-to-face services documented?

During the 30 days, the billing practitioner furnishes services as needed, such as:

- Obtaining and reviewing the discharge information
- Reviewing the need for, or following up on, pending tests and treatments
- Talking with other professionals who will care for the patient
- Educating the patient, family or caregiver
- Setting up referrals and community resources
- Helping schedule follow-up with other providers and services

Clinical staff, under the practitioner's general supervision, can communicate with the patient and
with agencies and community services, educate the patient on self-management, support treatment
and medication adherence, identify community resources, and help the patient get the care they need.

**Document:** each activity with the date, who did it and the outcome. A one-line entry per task is
enough. A note that only says "TCM coordination" is not.

## Was medication reconciliation done on time?

Medication reconciliation and management must happen **no later than the date of the face-to-face
visit**. This is the item most often missing in audits, because it gets done in conversation and
never written down.

**Document:** the reconciled medication list, discrepancies found against the discharge list, what
was changed, who did it and the date. If a pharmacist or nurse did the work before the visit, the
practitioner should review it at the visit and say so in the note.

## Does the face-to-face visit match the code?

The visit date and the MDM level together decide the code.

| Code | Medical decision making | Face-to-face visit due |
|---|---|---|
| 99495 | At least moderate complexity | Within 14 calendar days of discharge |
| 99496 | High complexity | Within 7 calendar days of discharge |

- **The window sets the ceiling.** A visit on day 9 cannot support 99496, even with high complexity
  decision making. It can only support 99495.
- **No visit inside 14 days, no TCM.** Bill the visit as the appropriate E/M service instead.
- **Telehealth counts.** Both codes are on the Medicare telehealth list. Record the modality.
- **Not on the discharge day.** If the same practitioner bills discharge day management, the required
  TCM visit cannot be on that same day.
- **The visit is part of TCM.** Do not bill the required visit again as a separate E/M service.
  Other medically necessary E/M visits during the 30 days can be billed separately.

**Document:** the visit date, the modality, and an MDM statement that shows the problems addressed,
the data reviewed and the risk, following the current AMA E/M guidelines.

## What stops you from billing TCM?

Check these before the claim goes out:

- The contact was missed and fewer than two timely attempts are documented.
- The visit fell outside the window for the code, or outside 14 days entirely.
- No medication reconciliation is recorded on or before the visit date.
- Another practitioner has already reported TCM for this discharge. Only one can, once per 30 days.
- Any part of the 30 days falls inside a global surgery period for a procedure the same practitioner
  billed.
- The patient was discharged to a setting that is not a community setting.

## Can you bill TCM with CCM, RPM or other services?

CMS allows several services in the same period when they are medically necessary and no time is
counted twice. These include [chronic care management](/services/chronic-care-management)
(99490, 99439, 99487, 99489, 99491), [remote patient monitoring](/services/remote-patient-monitoring),
[remote therapeutic monitoring](/services/remote-therapeutic-monitoring), care plan oversight
(G0181, G0182) and prolonged services without direct contact (99358, 99359).

**Document:** separate time logs for each service, and work that is clearly different. A call that
counts toward TCM coordination cannot also count toward the CCM minutes for the month. For the CCM
side of the record, see our [CPT 99490 documentation checklist](/resources/cpt-99490-documentation-checklist).

## When can you submit the claim?

The 30 day TCM period starts on the discharge date and runs for the next 29 days. CMS has allowed
practices to submit the claim once the face-to-face visit is done, rather than waiting for day 30.
Check your MAC's current guidance on the date of service to report before you set up the workflow.

## A day-by-day documentation timeline

| Day | Do this | Record this |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Learn of the discharge, open a TCM case | Discharge date, facility, source of the notice |
| 1 to 2 (business days) | Reach the patient or caregiver | Contact or each attempt, with date, staff and method |
| 2 to 7 | Get the discharge summary, reconcile medications, book the visit | Records reviewed, reconciled list, visit date booked |
| By 7 (99496) or 14 (99495) | Hold the face-to-face visit | Visit note, modality, MDM level and support |
| After the visit | Pre-bill chart review with this checklist | Any gaps fixed before the claim is sent |
| Through day 29 | Referrals, education, follow-up on tests | Each activity with date and outcome |

## How do you keep TCM documentation consistent?

Most TCM claims fail on day 0 or day 2, not at the visit. Three habits fix most of it:

1. **A discharge feed.** Decide how you hear about discharges: hospital alerts, the health
   information exchange or payer reports. A discharge you learn about on day 4 is already lost.
2. **One TCM template in the EHR.** Fields for the discharge date, contact attempts, medication
   reconciliation and visit date make the checklist part of the note.
3. **Protected visit slots.** Hold a few slots each week for discharge follow-ups so a high
   complexity patient can be seen inside 7 days. A [remote medical scheduler](/services/medical-scheduler)
   can manage those slots.
