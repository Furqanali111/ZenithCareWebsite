Most practices evaluating CCM for the first time ask the same
question: *can&rsquo;t we just add this to what our MAs already do?*
Sometimes yes. Often no. Here&rsquo;s the honest math for a typical
200-patient CCM panel and the three paths practices actually take.

## The baseline revenue

A 200-patient panel at the CPT 99490 base rate, $66/patient/month
in 2026, nets roughly **$13,200/month** in Medicare reimbursement
to the practice. Add realistic add-on and Complex CCM utilization and
the real number lands closer to **$15,000-$18,000/month**. That&rsquo;s
the ceiling every build-vs-buy comparison is working against.

## Path 1, in-house with existing staff

The seductive option: your MAs already know your patients, your EHR,
your workflows. Why pay a vendor?

**What it actually takes:**

- 200 patients × 20-25 minutes/month ≈ **70-85 clinical staff hours
  per month**, plus 20-30% overhead for scheduling, documentation,
  care-plan maintenance.
- One MA at $25/hour fully-loaded (wage + benefits + overhead)
  running this well: 100 hours/month ≈ **$2,500/month** in labor cost.
- Plus EHR configuration time, consent-outreach time, time-log
  software or process, and the opportunity cost of pulling that MA
  away from front-desk / in-visit work.

**Net:** $13,200 − $2,500 ≈ **$10,700/month**, before overhead.
Looks great on paper.

**The failure mode most practices hit:** CCM gets squeezed by
day-to-day urgent work. The 20-minute minimum per patient per month
slips. Documentation falls behind. Three months in, time logs are
sparse and the practice stops billing cleanly. Six months in, the
program is quietly shelved.

In-house CCM works when it&rsquo;s somebody&rsquo;s **actual job**, 
not a layer on top of an MA already running the front desk. That
usually means a dedicated hire.

## Path 2, hire a dedicated CCM coordinator

A dedicated full-time coordinator can run 200-400 patients
comfortably. Dedicated means CCM is their job description, not a
duty they fit in.

**What it actually takes:**

- A clinically-oriented coordinator (RN, LPN, or trained non-licensed
  staff supervised by a physician): **$55,000-$75,000/year** fully
  loaded, plus benefits ≈ $70,000-$95,000.
- Hiring time: 2-4 months in most U.S. markets for anyone remotely
  qualified.
- Training ramp: 6-8 weeks before they&rsquo;re net-positive on
  billing hours.
- Management overhead: 2-4 hours/week of your clinical lead&rsquo;s
  time for ongoing supervision.

**Net for a 200-patient panel:** $13,200 × 12 = $158,400 annual
revenue minus $85,000 fully-loaded = **~$73,000/year** net. Better
margin at scale (400 patients): ~$230,000 revenue minus the same
~$85,000 = ~$145,000/year.

**Where this breaks:** panel size has to grow fast enough to justify
the hire, and the coordinator has to stay. CCM coordinator turnover
is brutal, the role is repetitive, patient-facing, and
documentation-heavy. Replacing one wipes out 6-8 weeks of billing.

## Path 3, outsource to a specialist vendor

A dedicated CCM vendor runs the clinical work under your billing
physician&rsquo;s &ldquo;incident to&rdquo; supervision. Your practice bills Medicare;
the vendor invoices you for the labor.

**What it actually takes:**

- Vendor fee: typically **$35-$55 per enrolled patient per month**,
  flat per-patient-per-month, not a percentage of your Medicare
  collections.
- BAA signed before any patient data moves.
- EHR access provisioned for the vendor&rsquo;s clinical team.
- 3-6 weeks of onboarding (contracting, training, pilot).

**Net for a 200-patient panel at $45 per patient:** $13,200 revenue
minus $9,000 vendor fee = **$4,200/month net**, no hiring risk, no
turnover exposure.

Lower absolute margin than Path 2. But: no capital at risk, no
hiring cycle, no ramp. The economics scale cleanly, 400 patients at
$45 nets ~$8,400/month without an additional management burden.

## The decision isn&rsquo;t about which number is biggest

It&rsquo;s about which risk you can carry.

- **Path 2 (hire) has the biggest upside** but requires a hire you can
  commit to, panel growth to justify them, and management bandwidth
  you may not have.
- **Path 1 (existing staff) has the smallest apparent cost** but the
  highest failure rate in practice, it&rsquo;s where CCM programs go
  to die quietly.
- **Path 3 (outsource) is the lowest-risk, lowest-margin path.** Right
  for practices that want the revenue without the operational
  distraction.

Most practices that end up successful on CCM start with Path 3 for
the first 12-18 months, prove the economics in their panel, and then
decide whether to bring it in-house (Path 2) once the volume justifies
a dedicated FTE.
